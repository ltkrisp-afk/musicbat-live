# MusicBat Direct Licensing Agreement

Version: draft-2026-10-29.1
Status: COMPLETE REVIEW DRAFT — NOT APPROVED, NOT EFFECTIVE, NOT AVAILABLE FOR ACCEPTANCE.

This document and Schedules A–F form one proposed agreement. Bracketed activation particulars in Schedule A must be completed, the identified legal and commercial decisions approved, and an approved version separately issued before acceptance is enabled. Displaying this draft, uploading test data, or checking a test checkbox does not execute it. The operative clauses below describe the agreement proposed for that later approved version.

## 1. Parties, purpose, and formation

The agreement is between the legal operator identified in Schedule A ("MusicBat") and the person or entity identified there ("Artist"). An individual acting for a band, company, publisher, or other rights holder signs only in the capacity and within the authority documented in Schedules A and B. An account name or stage name alone does not identify the contracting person.

MusicBat manages the discovery and sale of authorized music, not the musician’s career. MusicBat is a music-discovery and digital-commerce platform. It is not an artist-management company, record label, publisher, or general sync-licensing representative. Artist remains independent, retains creative direction, and may pursue opportunities elsewhere.

Only an approved version expressly accepted by Artist becomes binding. Electronic acceptance identifies the authenticated account, signer's legal name and capacity, exact agreement version and incorporated schedules, affirmative action, server timestamp, and associated recording. Opening the review dialog is encouraged but is not a condition of acceptance. Silence, continued browsing, a prechecked box, artist success, or a subscription purchase is not acceptance. Each additional recording requires its own accurate Schedule B and recorded acceptance. MusicBat supplies a retainable copy of the accepted terms and completed schedules. No administrator may fabricate Artist's acceptance.

## 2. Ownership and independence

Artist and the relevant third parties retain their respective copyrights, neighboring rights, ownership shares, names, likenesses, and other intellectual property. This agreement transfers no copyright ownership, creates no work made for hire, assigns no publishing interest, and confers no lien, option, right of first refusal, or exclusivity. A platform revenue allocation is not a copyright ownership allocation.

All licenses are nonexclusive, limited to the expressly authorized services, uses, territory, and term, and subject to sections 3–6. Artist may distribute and license the same music elsewhere, subject to Artist's own existing obligations. No restriction may be added merely because a recording becomes successful. Any proposed exclusive right requires a separate, specifically negotiated affirmative agreement; none is granted here.

## 3. Recordings, compositions, and authority

"Recording" means the particular sound recording, version, and file identified in Schedule B. "Composition" means each underlying musical work and lyrics embodied in it. A Recording license does not itself license a Composition. Each relevant master owner, songwriter, co-owner, publisher, administrator, label, performer, producer, and authorized representative must be identified where that person's interest or contract affects the grant or payment.

Artist must distinguish ownership from administration, agency, distribution, and collection authority. Artist may grant only rights Artist owns or is presently authorized to license for the stated use, share, territory, and period. A collaborator's participation, silence, credit, friendship, or membership in a band is not authorization. A distribution agreement is not assumed to include sublicensing or composition authority. Disclosure of a co-owner does not bind that co-owner.

Schedule B lists ownership shares separately for the Recording and each Composition, including unknown or disputed interests. The intended coverage must reach all rights necessary for the proposed use. Unverified, conflicting, or missing shares remain unresolved; the system must not infer authority over the remainder. A narrower grant is usable only if counsel confirms it legally permits the specific service and a reviewed schedule records that conclusion.

Artist must disclose covers, samples, interpolations, remixes, beats, production-library material, featured performances, artwork, and other controlled material, with the applicable permissions and restrictions. Artist must not assert that a mechanical permission alone clears a sampled master or public performance. Public-domain and other exception claims require a documented basis and territorial review; they are not automatically accepted.

## 4. PROs, publishers, and existing obligations

Schedule B records applicable performing-rights organization or collecting-society affiliations, legal names, IPI/CAE numbers if assigned and known, publisher/administrator details, and the rights and territories administered. An unknown identifier is recorded as unknown; one is never invented. "None" is an affirmative declaration of no applicable affiliation, not a substitute for missing information.

An affiliation, IPI/CAE number, ISRC, or ISWC identifies information; it is not a license, proof of title, or proof that direct licensing is permitted. Artist supplies relevant publishing, administration, label, distribution, collective-management, union, and other restrictions, and any required notices, approvals, or direct-license carve-outs. MusicBat evaluates the actual coverage and keeps an affected use unavailable until required permissions and terms are documented. This agreement neither cancels existing obligations nor presumes that direct licensing eliminates every reporting or statutory duty. MusicBat does not adopt a separate statutory radio licensing model by this agreement and will not charge duplicate licensing amounts for the same rights and uses already validly covered.

## 5. Limited service authorization

Subject to complete rights clearance and the applicable compensation conditions in Schedule C, Artist grants MusicBat the following rights solely for the identified Recordings and covered Compositions:

(a) receive, securely store, back up, reproduce, and technically transcode files as reasonably necessary to review submissions and perform an authorized service; technical processing may change format or loudness for delivery but must not create a creative remix or materially misrepresent the work;

(b) digitally transmit and publicly perform authorized Recordings and publicly perform covered Compositions through interactive listening and discovery features expressly identified in Schedule C, including free listening or expanded-listening membership only when that use is separately enabled there; make only the reproductions and temporary delivery copies reasonably necessary for those authorized streams;

(c) reproduce and distribute authorized permanent digital downloads to purchasing customers, including fulfillment and lawful replacement delivery under section 10; offer the recording at the Artist-approved price, receive payment through the disclosed payment provider, and account under section 7;

(d) display Artist-supplied names, approved credits, artwork, descriptions, and factual release information in MusicBat profiles, search, discovery, catalog listings, and transaction records to identify the music and explain its availability. Audio previews, external promotional audio, and other public promotional uses require the specific clearance and compensation treatment recorded in Schedule C. No implied endorsement of a sponsor is permitted.

MusicBat may use hosting, storage, content-delivery, payment, and other necessary service providers solely to perform these obligations, under access and confidentiality controls appropriate to their work. Providers receive no independent exploitation rights. Customer download rights are limited to personal, noncommercial listening and reasonable personal backup and device transfers; no redistribution, resale, public performance, sync, or sublicensing is granted. Customers retain rights independently provided by law.

Territories and start/end dates are expressly listed in Schedule C. An empty territory is not worldwide permission. MusicBat must restrict access to the authorized scope or keep the affected use disabled if it cannot enforce the restriction. This contract permits a service only after clearance; it does not represent that every permitted feature has been built or launched.

## 6. Excluded rights and optional services

No synchronization with film, advertising, games, video, or other audiovisual works; creative remix; derivative-work commission; merchandise; publishing administration; collection mandate; training of generative models; voice cloning; sale of stems; or unrelated exploitation is granted. No general agency, career-management appointment, booking authority, label relationship, or authority to negotiate third-party opportunities is created. Ordinary technical processing under section 5 is the sole technical exception and conveys no creative exploitation right.

MusicBat may later offer optional artist services. Each requires separate terms identifying services, permissions, compensation, duration, termination, and affirmative acceptance. Artist may decline without forfeiting existing platform rights or accrued earnings. Popularity, sales, votes, chart position, or other success never triggers enrollment, exclusivity, additional commission, ownership transfer, or management authority. A dashboard indication of interest in future licensing or compilations is not a license for that activity.

## 7. Digital purchases and payment accounting

For an authorized permanent download, the recording's base sale price, excluding separately stated taxes and buyer processing charges, is allocated 85% to Artist and 15% to MusicBat. The Artist amount is rounded to the nearest currency minor unit and the MusicBat amount is the remainder, so the two sum to the base price. For a $2.00 base price, Artist receives $1.70 and MusicBat $0.30. Historical transaction allocations are preserved as originally recorded and are not rewritten by this agreement.

Applicable processing fees are paid by the buyer and disclosed separately before confirmation, subject to applicable law and payment-provider rules. Taxes are shown and handled as legally required; taxes are not platform revenue or artist compensation. MusicBat must validate the processing-charge method and disclosures before enabling the affected payment method. An estimate must be identified as an estimate; undisclosed surcharges or automatic deductions from the 85% Artist amount are not authorized here. Any unavoidable unrecovered processing expense is not silently shifted to Artist.

The designated recipient must complete the payment provider's identity and payout requirements and demonstrate authority to receive the funds. Verified payout identity does not prove copyright ownership. Schedule D separates master ownership, composition ownership, and allocation of the Artist's 85% among recipients. MusicBat currently pays the designated verified recipient; it does not promise automated multi-party split payouts. A representative receiving the Artist pool must have documented collection authority and must account to the other beneficiaries under their signed allocation instructions. Without complete payment instructions, affected new sales remain unavailable; earned funds are not forfeited.

No duplicate percentages, platform fees, unspecified reserves, or unilateral split changes may be added. Any separate license payment necessary for a purchase must have a written source of payment and payee approved before that use is enabled, consistent with the 85/15 and buyer-processing policy. No promise in this agreement obligates a third party to accept another person's allocation.

Payment timing follows the verified processor's disclosed settlement and payout schedule, recorded in Schedule D before activation, with no MusicBat-created minimum or holdback unless separately agreed in writing. MusicBat records payment identifiers, base price, artist and platform amounts, processing charge, taxes, currency, payment status, recipient, and entitlement. It provides Artist access to understandable statements and a reasonable correction process. Neither payout availability nor instant settlement is guaranteed when a provider or law imposes a documented restriction.

Refunds, duplicate payments, fraud, chargebacks, failed fulfillment, and legal remedies are handled under applicable law and disclosed customer terms. Reversal of a base price reverses the corresponding 85/15 allocation proportionately and is recorded against the original transaction; it does not rewrite that transaction. Disputed or unrecoverable processing charges must be separately identified and are not automatically taken from Artist's share. A temporary hold must be limited to the amount reasonably in dispute or legally required, explained when lawful, reviewed promptly, and released when its basis ends. Undisputed amounts remain payable. No blanket "all sales final" clause overrides mandatory customer rights.

## 8. Listening membership and other compensation

Artist registration, submission, and authorized publication are free; Artist need not purchase a membership. Basic listening remains free. The separate expanded-listening membership costs listeners $5 per month, with its features, renewal, cancellation, and applicable taxes disclosed in customer terms. Membership buys access to features; it does not convey ownership or establish permission to use unlicensed music.

This agreement creates no artist share of membership revenue and no unstated streaming royalty, rate, minimum guarantee, or royalty-free grant. Compensation for streaming, audio previews, public promotional audio, and every licensed use outside the established digital-purchase allocation remains subject to separately agreed written terms in Schedule C. Silence, "TBD," a blank, or zero entered without express agreement means NOT AGREED and that use stays disabled. If the parties later choose a zero monetary rate or other consideration, it must be expressly described and affirmatively agreed by every required licensor; it is never presumed. The private submission/review and necessary recordkeeping permission is incidental to evaluation of Artist's request, creates no public exploitation permission, and imposes no upload fee.

## 9. Representations, review, and continuing duties

Artist represents that the submitted declarations are accurate and complete to Artist's knowledge after reasonable inquiry; Artist has legal capacity and documented authority for each granted right; required co-owner and third-party approvals are authentic and sufficient; supplied materials and authorized uses do not violate disclosed contractual restrictions or third-party rights; and Artist will promptly report a claim, rights change, expiry, revocation, or error. Artist must not conceal known gaps or represent uncertain rights as cleared.

MusicBat will use the content only within the approved scope, maintain reasonable security and access controls, keep accurate consent and transaction evidence, apply restrictions and withdrawal controls, account for funds received, and investigate credible rights complaints. It may request reasonably necessary documentation and suspend affected uses during review. Neither MusicBat's acceptance of a file nor its rights review guarantees title, registers copyright, or substitutes for legal clearance. Artist supplies authorized evidence through the designated private review channel, not public catalog descriptions. MusicBat restricts evidence access and does not publish contracts or sensitive identity information in the catalog.

For shared, externally controlled, restricted, disputed, or unknown rights, the reviewer must inspect supporting authorization and document the rights, shares, uses, territory, term, compensation, signer authority, and review decision. A checkbox, URL, or uploaded document by itself cannot set a clearance state. Approved changes require a new linked schedule or review event; prior evidence is retained.

## 10. Withdrawal, termination, and existing customers

Artist may request withdrawal of a Recording or termination through the account controls or the notice contact in Schedule A. MusicBat disables new sales and new public streaming promptly upon receipt and prevents new transactions from withdrawn offers. Pending payment sessions must be expired or reconciled using their actual payment state; already completed lawful transactions are not silently erased. MusicBat may suspend or terminate affected uses for a material breach, credible infringement risk, security issue, legal requirement, or discontinuation of the service. It gives the reason and opportunity to correct when lawful and practicable; urgent protection need not wait for a cure period.

Termination ends prospective public exploitation permissions. The limited nonexclusive permission necessary to honor lawful purchases completed before withdrawal survives for those customers: retain the purchased file and purchase evidence, deliver any outstanding lawful purchase, and provide secure replacement downloads for that customer's personal use, including legitimate cross-device access. This survival permission must be included in every necessary underlying authorization. It permits no new sale, general stream, promotion, or enlarged customer license. MusicBat does not promise perpetual service operation; before discontinuing delivery where practicable, it gives affected customers notice and a reasonable opportunity to retain their downloads and addresses mandatory remedies.

No clause requires delivery of infringing content or conduct contrary to law, a court order, or a demonstrated lack of authority. In that case MusicBat may restrict delivery, preserve entitlement and evidence, notify affected customers when lawful, and provide required remedies. A refusal of lawful continued delivery must be documented, not concealed as deletion of a purchase. Accrued payment duties, ownership protections, lawful purchase permissions, disputes, confidentiality, and necessary records survive to the extent required to perform them. Ordinary withdrawal alone is not a ground to confiscate earnings or customers' lawful copies.

## 11. Complaints and infringement procedures

MusicBat maintains a publicly identified copyright contact and, where applicable, a properly designated and registered DMCA agent. It evaluates notices and counter-notices under applicable law, acts expeditiously on valid notices, notifies affected parties when required, and preserves the relevant evidence. A counter-notice does not automatically restore access when a legal restraint or unresolved contractual clearance issue remains. The statutory restoration process and timing apply where their conditions are satisfied. MusicBat maintains and reasonably implements the applicable repeat-infringer policy and accommodates qualifying standard technical measures.

This agreement does not promise DMCA safe-harbor eligibility or immunize either party from its own conduct. Artist may dispute a claim and supply evidence; MusicBat does not adjudicate copyright ownership merely by changing catalog availability. Competing claims trigger a documented hold on the affected use or disputed proceeds, with undisputed rights and sums handled separately.

## 12. Records, privacy, and amendments

MusicBat retains the exact accepted agreement body, checkbox wording, version identifier, linked completed declarations, authenticated account identity, server acceptance time, recording association, authorization evidence references, review events, and financial and entitlement records. Corrections and amendments are append-only linked events; the original acceptance is not overwritten or backdated. MusicBat supplies Artist an accessible copy and export of Artist's relevant records, subject to third-party confidentiality and lawful verification.

Retention is limited to fulfilling purchases, accounting, rights provenance, legal obligations, and reasonably necessary claims defense under the approved retention schedule. Legal holds are documented. Retention is not permission to retain unrelated personal data indefinitely or to ignore applicable deletion, access, or correction rights. A deletion request is evaluated against surviving lawful obligations and the reason for any limited retention is explained. The privacy notice identifies categories, purposes, service providers, safeguards, and applicable rights. Sensitive evidence is accessible only to authorized personnel and providers who need it.

A material change to permissions, compensation, liability, or dispute terms requires a new approved version and affirmative acceptance before it governs new affected activity. It does not retroactively alter an existing purchase, copyright share, or accepted financial allocation. Clerical corrections are documented and cannot expand rights. The version and schedule governing each transaction remain traceable.

## 13. Indemnification

To the extent permitted by applicable law, Artist will indemnify MusicBat against amounts finally awarded to a third party, or agreed in a settlement approved under this section, and reasonable defense costs, to the extent caused by Artist's material breach of the rights/authority representations, knowing submission of infringing material, fraud, or willful misconduct. Artist is not responsible under this clause for MusicBat's unauthorized uses, modifications outside the grant, negligence, or misconduct, or for a claim to the extent caused by those acts.

MusicBat will indemnify Artist on the same basis for third-party claims to the extent caused by MusicBat's exploitation outside the granted rights, fraud, or willful misconduct. Neither party must fund an unsupported demand merely because it is asserted. The claimant must be promptly notified by the indemnified party; delayed notice reduces protection only to the extent of material prejudice. The indemnifying party may control the defense with competent counsel reasonably acceptable to the other, with reasonable cooperation at the indemnifying party's expense. No settlement may admit fault, impose nonmonetary duties, surrender rights, or require payment by the other party without that party's written consent. Conflicted interests permit separate counsel with reasonable cost allocation according to responsibility. This clause is subject to section 14 and mandatory law.

## 14. Service standards and liability

MusicBat does not guarantee audience size, discovery placement, earnings, career outcomes, uninterrupted availability, or prevention of all unauthorized copying. It remains responsible for the express security, scope, accounting, withdrawal, and delivery obligations it assumes. Artist remains responsible for its own truthful declarations and authorized grants. Neither party excludes a duty that cannot lawfully be excluded.

To the extent permitted by law, neither party is liable to the other for speculative lost opportunities or indirect or consequential loss, except where that exclusion would defeat an express payment or indemnification obligation or apply to fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence, unauthorized exploitation of intellectual property, or a confidentiality/security breach. Liability for unpaid earned sums, customer entitlements and mandatory remedies is not extinguished. No aggregate monetary liability cap is established in this draft; any cap requires express owner and counsel approval in a later version. This agreement does not bind third-party rights holders or eliminate their remedies.

## 15. Notices, disputes, and governing law

Contract notices use the verified contacts in Schedule A. Account notices may supplement them but must not replace a legally required method. Parties keep their contacts current and retain sending/receipt evidence. Before ordinary litigation, a party will provide a clear description of the dispute and reasonably attempt good-faith resolution; this does not bar urgent relief, mandatory statutory remedies, or filing necessary to preserve a limitation period.

The governing jurisdiction and court venue must be completed in Schedule A before this agreement is activated, consistent with the actual operator, participating parties, and mandatory law. Applicable federal copyright law and nonwaivable local protections continue to apply. This draft contains no arbitration mandate, class-action waiver, or contractual shortening of limitation periods. No dispute forum is inferred from an account's location or MusicBat's branding.

## 16. General terms and order of precedence

The parties are independent contracting parties. No employment, partnership, joint venture, fiduciary career-management relationship, or general agency is created. The limited authority to process authorized sales and payments does not permit MusicBat to bind Artist to other deals.

Neither party may assign broader rights than it holds. MusicBat may transfer this agreement with the service only to a successor that assumes its obligations in writing, preserves rights and purchase records, gives reasonable notice, and does not expand exploitation; Artist retains prospective withdrawal rights. Any assignment requiring a rights holder's approval remains subject to that approval. Artist may change its representative or successor payee only after authority and payment instructions are verified; this does not transfer others' copyright or defeat accrued obligations.

This agreement and its expressly incorporated completed schedules are the entire agreement for their defined subject. Specific signed schedules may narrow scope and specify permitted compensation but cannot silently override the 85/15 purchase allocation, add excluded rights, or create exclusivity. Such a change requires an explicitly identified and separately approved amendment. Mandatory law prevails. If a provision is unenforceable, it is severed only to the necessary extent; no severance enlarges the license. Failure to enforce once is not waiver. No force-majeure event excuses accounting for funds already received or permits unlicensed exploitation. Counterparts and reliable electronic signatures are permitted to the extent applicable law recognizes them.
